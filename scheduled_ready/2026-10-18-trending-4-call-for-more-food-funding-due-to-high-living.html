A cost-of-living squeeze? Distributional implications of rising inflation

The cost-of-living squeeze is a pressing issue in many OECD countries, with rising inflation exacerbating the financial burden on households. This phenomenon disproportionately affects vulnerable groups, particularly those with high energy and food expenditures. According to the OECD Economics Department working paper, the decline in purchasing power between August 2021 and August 2022 varied significantly across countries, ranging from 3% in Japan to 18% in the Czech Republic. This decline is primarily attributed to energy prices, with Denmark, Italy, and the United Kingdom experiencing the most significant increases.

OECD Economics Department working papers In countries where inflation is more broadly based, such as the Czech Republic and the United States, energy prices play a lesser role in the impact on households' purchasing power. Across all considered countries, inflation weighs more heavily on low-income households compared to high-income households. Rural households are particularly affected, often more than low-income ones, due to higher energy price inflation. To mitigate the impact of rising inflation, especially on energy prices, targeted income and price support measures are essential.

One of the central problems of current food systems is that many of the costs of harmful foods are externalized, i.e., are not reflected in market prices. At the same time, the benefits of healthful foods are not appreciated. Due to externalities, sustainable and healthy food is often less affordable to consumers and less profitable for businesses than unsustainable and unhealthy food.
