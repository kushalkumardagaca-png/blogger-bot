This paper aims to construct a multidimensional FI index to measure the level of FI across 91 countries, considering various income groups. The principal component analysis method is employed to address the criticism of arbitrary weight selection and to provide a deeper reflection of financial inclusion. The data used comes from the International Monetary Fund (IMF) Financial Access Survey (FAS), the World Development Indicators (World Bank), and the Global Findex Database, covering the period from 2004 to 2020.

Journal of risk and financial management The paper's approach is innovative in that it considers 13 indicators across three dimensions of FI: access to financial services, financial literacy, and financial awareness. Unlike previous work, this study considers both developing and developed countries, providing a comprehensive comparison. The proposed index has several advantages. Firstly, it is robust, comparable across countries, and has good predictive power in tracking household microeconomic indicators, such as accounts and savings.

Additionally, it is well correlated with macroeconomic variables like literacy rate, poverty, GINI index, real interest rate, and employers.
