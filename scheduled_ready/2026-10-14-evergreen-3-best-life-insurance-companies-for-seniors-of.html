LEGAL PROTECTION FOR INSURANCE COMPANIES AGAINST ABUSE OF INSURANCE CLAIMS

Legal Protection for Insurance Companies Against Abuse of Insurance Claims Source: the DOI recordIn the realm of insurance, the relationship between insurers and insureds is often fraught with potential for abuse. This phenomenon, known as moral hazard, can lead to significant economic losses for insurance companies and undermine the integrity of the insurance system. As a form of risk transfer, insurance compensates for losses due to uncertain events such as accidents, fires, or death. However, in practice, this relationship is often intertwined with the issue of moral hazard, particularly the misuse of claims by insured individuals who act dishonestly.

This paper examines the forms of legal protection available to insurance companies to address these manipulative practices. The legal framework in Indonesia provides a balance of rights and obligations in insurance contracts, but it is not always sufficient to prevent misuse of claims. This paper aims to provide a deeper understanding of the importance of maintaining a balance of rights and obligations in insurance contracts and the need to strengthen legal aspects in anticipation of such misuse. Through a normative legal approach, this paper explores the forms of legal protection available, both through contractual instruments (internal) and external regulations such as Law Number 40 of 2014 concerning Insurance.

Supply and Demand Imbalances in the Indonesian Life Insurance Industry

In Indonesia, the life insurance industry has faced significant challenges in providing adequate life insurance protection for retirees. The retired group is considered high risk due to age and health factors, making it difficult for them to obtain life insurance protection from insurance companies. This scarcity of life insurance protection is a major concern, as it affects the retirement savings of many individuals.

The research method used in this study is an explanatory quantitative approach, combining surveys of insurance companies and financial institutions with mediation analysis (moral hazard) and moderation (fraud and risk management capacity). The results of the study highlight that supply-demand imbalances, high claims ratios, limited capital, and weak reinsurance support are the main factors causing the scarcity of life insurance protection for retirees' credit.

The role of moral hazard and fraud also increases the risk of a scarcity of protection. This study concludes that collaboration between insurance companies, banks, and regulators is essential to create an inclusive and sustainable life insurance protection model for the retiree segment.
