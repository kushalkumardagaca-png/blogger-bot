The influence of stock index futures on stock market prices is a topic of significant interest in the financial community. This paper, based on theoretical analysis and empirical tests from both the Chinese and overseas markets, aims to explore the impacts of the introduction of stock index futures on stock market prices. The launch of Shanghai-Shenzhen 300 index futures on April 16, 2010, marked a pivotal moment in China's securities market, ushering in a new era of short-position trading. This development is crucial for investors' investment concepts, structures, behaviors, and strategies, fundamentally altering how they perceive and participate in the stock market. Source: the DOI record

Theoretical analysis in this paper delves into the mechanisms through which stock index futures influence stock market prices. It suggests that the introduction of index futures can lead to a shift in market expectations, potentially affecting the volatility and liquidity of the stock market. For instance, the expectation of future price movements can influence current market prices, leading to a more pronounced impact on short-term price fluctuations. This mechanism underscores the importance of understanding how market participants interpret and react to new market instruments.

Empirical tests of the experiences in both the Chinese and overseas markets provide valuable insights into the practical implications of stock index futures. In China, the paper observes that the introduction of index futures has led to a more diversified investment approach among investors. This diversification is evident in the increased use of short positions, which can be seen as a form of hedging against market risks. The empirical tests also reveal that the launch of index futures has not only affected the investment landscape but has also influenced the overall investment behavior of Chinese investors, moving them towards a more systematic and informed approach to market participation.

In the overseas markets, the paper finds that the introduction of index futures has had a more pronounced impact on the pricing dynamics of stock markets. This is evidenced by the increased volatility and liquidity in the market, as investors are more likely to engage in hedging strategies. The empirical tests also highlight the importance of informational and behavioral effects in stock prices, suggesting that the introduction of index futures has provided new evidence on how market participants interpret and react to price signals. The paper concludes by emphasizing the importance of gaining a comprehensive understanding of China's unique financial system.

This includes recognizing the distinctive features of China's capital market, such as the role of state-owned enterprises and the influence of regulatory policies. It also highlights the need to avoid overgeneralizing research findings from the US setting, which may not always apply to China's market conditions.
