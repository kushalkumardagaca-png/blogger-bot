Exploring direct support work through autoethnographic fiction

Exploring Direct Support Work Through Autoethnographic Fiction Source: the archived record

In this project, I present a novella, constructed as a piece of autoethnographic fiction rooted in my experience working in a waiver home in rural Indiana. A waiver home is a residential setting with twenty-four-hour staffing, where one to five people live with intellectual and, in some cases, physical disabilities. The primary purpose of this project is to produce a piece of fiction that accurately portrays the experience of people who work in waiver homes in rural Indiana. Additionally, the project aims to provide a close reading of parts of the fiction using preexisting theory, to demonstrate the role power plays in the dynamics of waiver home life.

The structure of this project is as follows: I discuss how I came to write this piece of fiction, first in a "General Background" section, then in more detail in the literature review, where I introduce the texts I utilize in the analysis. Following the literature review, I discuss my personal positioning in relation to the issues and topics I discuss and explore in this project. Then, I present the piece of fiction. Following the piece of fiction, I analyze the role of bio-power, risk, and emotion in the fiction. Finally, I present a reflection on notable issues not explored by this project.

Budget 2025: A Call for Reform

The November 26th Budget 2025 is anticipated to come with a significant downgrade to the fiscal outlook, with pressures from recent policy reversals, rising debt-interest costs, and a gloomier economic outlook. It is plausible that annual borrowing could be £20-£40 billion, or more, above the Office for Budget Responsibility's March forecast. This is undoubtedly a challenging outlook for the Government, but it is one that it must respond to robustly. This report considers how tax policy can be used to address the expected shortfall. It sets out a range of options that could together raise over £30 billion, while minimising the economic impact of higher taxes on the economy and living standards. It recommends a strategic approach to reforming the UK's often-distortionary £1 trillion tax system: supporting fair competition between firms; removing biases against workers; and aligning taxes with social and environmental goals. This Budget could simultaneously provide fiscal reassurance, a meaningful Child Poverty Strategy, and long-lasting beneficial tax reform.

Despite these challenges, the internet has provided a space where writers can express their creativity and passion, and where they can find a sense of validation and belonging.
