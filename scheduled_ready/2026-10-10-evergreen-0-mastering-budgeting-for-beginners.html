Financial Glossary

Financial Literacy
The combination of theoretical knowledge, practical skills, behaviors, and attitudes regarding budgeting, saving, investing, and managing credit and debt.
Financial Inclusion
Access to and usage of financial services and digital tools that help expand economic participation and reduce poverty.
Financial Self-Efficacy
An individual's internal confidence and psychological resilience in their ability to execute sound financial management behaviors over the long term.
Fixed Expenses
Mandatory recurring financial commitments that remain stable month after month, such as housing rent, mortgage payments, utility bills, insurance premiums, and loan obligations.
Variable Expenses
Discretionary outlays or costs that are not fixed, such as spending on dining out or entertainment.
Net Take-Home Pay
The true baseline of incoming resources calculated after deductions for taxes, insurance premiums, and mandatory retirement contributions are made from gross salary.
FinTech
Modern financial technology, digital platforms, and mobile applications that provide banking, credit services, automated tracking, and portfolio management.
Debt Management
The application of financial knowledge, skills, and cautious borrowing attitudes to handle credit obligations and avoid high-interest burdens.
Risk Perception
Cognitive attitudes and awareness regarding risk and reward that influence early investment choices and moderate the impact of financial literacy.
Emergency Fund
A robust savings reserve established to mitigate unexpected economic shocks and prevent reliance on high-interest debt instruments.
Budget Deficit
A shortfall where expenses or debt obligations exceed available income, forcing reliance on borrowing.
Asset Allocation
The transition from short-term expense tracking to long-term placement of capital into various investment vehicles and portfolios.
Cognition
Mental processing and awareness dimensions that interact with financial literacy to shape investment choices and financial inclusion.
Social Capital
The social dimensions involving collective action, bonding, bridging, and trust associated with investor behavior and financial networks.
Financial Behavior
How individuals routinely act on their monetary choices and daily spending habits.
Investment Decision
The process of allocating surplus capital into market instruments, heavily steered by financial literacy, risk perceptions, and self-efficacy.
