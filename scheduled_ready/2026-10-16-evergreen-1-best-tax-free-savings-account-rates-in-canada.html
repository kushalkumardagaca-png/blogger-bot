In Canada, tax-free savings accounts (TFSA) have been a significant financial tool since January 2009. These accounts offer a tax-preferred environment for individuals to save and invest without incurring tax on the contributions or earnings. The primary goal of TFSA is to encourage saving and long-term investment without the immediate tax burden.

One of the key features of TFSA is the tax-free compounding of investment returns. This means that any interest or capital gains earned within the account are not subject to income tax. This feature has been a significant driver of TFSA's popularity, as it allows individuals to grow their savings without the immediate tax implications. However, the effectiveness of TFSA in promoting savings can be questioned. A study by TSpace suggests that the TFSA regime is not likely to significantly incentivize new savings. This implies that the primary purpose of TFSA is not to encourage new savings but to provide a tax-free environment for existing savings.
