This is where the concept of a "collective agency" comes into play. The collective agency theory posits that financial systems are not just about technical functionality but also about the trust, coordination, and legitimacy among competing financial institutions.

SWIFT, for example, is a cooperative agency that facilitates cross-border payments and is essential to the stability and legitimacy of international finance. The cross-border payments system requires not only technical functionality but also widespread trust, coordination, and legitimacy among competing financial institutions. SWIFT's cooperative governance structure uniquely facilitates these elements, making it the ideal solution for a global coordinating authority.
