Advocates have argued that these laws could facilitate multigenerational living, but the extent of this impact remains unclear.

To address this gap, researchers examined the relationship between state ADU reforms and multigenerational living in California. The study, published in the Journal of the American Planning Association, utilized parcel-level data from 14 California counties, covering approximately two-thirds of the state's population. The analysis linked permit records for ADUs with geocoded voter registration and consumer reference data, identifying multigenerational households based on shared surnames and age differences across single-family parcels. The findings revealed that parcels with ADU permits are, on average, slightly less likely to be multigenerational than comparable parcels without permits, by roughly 2 to 3 percentage points after controlling for parcel and neighborhood characteristics.

This negative association persists among owner-occupied parcels. The study suggests that while ADU legalization alone does not significantly promote multigenerational living, planners seeking to facilitate this arrangement should consider complementary measures such as preapproved plans and alternative financing methods.
